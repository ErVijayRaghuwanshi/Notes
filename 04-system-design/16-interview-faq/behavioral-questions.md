# 🤝 Behavioral Questions for System Design Interviews

These questions help you practice the leadership and collaboration side of architecture discussions.

---

**Q1: Describe a time you made a trade-off between speed of delivery and long-term architecture.**  
Show how you scoped the decision, what risk you accepted, and how you planned future cleanup.

**Q2: Tell me about a time you simplified an over-engineered design.**  
Focus on how you recognized unnecessary complexity and aligned the team on a simpler path.

**Q3: Describe a design disagreement with another engineer or team.**  
Explain how you compared options, used evidence, and preserved collaboration while making the call.

**Q4: Tell me about a production incident that changed how you design systems.**  
Highlight the root cause, what you learned, and what design or operational improvement followed.

**Q5: Describe a time you improved reliability without adding a lot of new infrastructure.**  
Good answers often involve timeouts, caching, backpressure, better observability, or clearer ownership.

**Q6: Tell me about a migration you led or influenced.**  
Cover stakeholder communication, compatibility strategy, rollout steps, and risk management.

**Q7: Describe a time you had to make a system design decision with incomplete data.**  
Show your assumptions, how you reduced risk, and what signals you watched after launch.

**Q8: Tell me about a time you balanced one large customer's needs against the platform for everyone else.**  
This is a great prompt for multi-tenancy, fairness, and product judgment.

**Q9: Describe a design you would change if you had more time.**  
Be honest, specific, and focused on the highest-leverage improvement.

**Q10: How do you make sure newer engineers understand a complex design?**  
Talk about diagrams, written docs, design reviews, walkthroughs, and operational examples.
